### <img src="https://github.com/dan0dev/ScreenPrompt/raw/main/assets/icon-source-512.png" width="26" align="top" alt="" /> ScreenPrompt

> **Ticket #001** &nbsp;·&nbsp; **The problem:** looking away at your notes during a call or recording makes you seem unprepared.
>
> ![Status: resolved](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-resolved-2ea44f?style=flat-square)

Your notes, scripts, and reminders: visible to you, invisible to everyone else.

A transparent, always-on-top overlay for Windows. You can read it; screen shares and recordings can't. It's excluded from capture in OBS, Zoom, Teams, and Windows' own Snipping Tool, so your notes stay on screen during a call or stream without anyone seeing them. Runs 100% locally, no telemetry.

The first version was written in Python; the current one runs on Tauri 2.

### <img src="https://github.com/dan0dev/perch/raw/main/assets/perch.svg" width="26" align="top" alt="" /> Perch

> **Ticket #002** &nbsp;·&nbsp; **The problem:** a Windows snip only lands on the clipboard, so it can be pasted but not dragged into an upload box.
>
> ![Status: resolved](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-resolved-2ea44f?style=flat-square)

A temporary screenshot shelf for Windows. Snips land, perch for a while, then fly off.

<kbd>Win</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd> only puts a screenshot on the clipboard, so you can paste it but you can't drag it. Perch catches every snip, shelves it as a card at the edge of your screen, and lets you drag one or a whole batch into any app or upload box as real PNG files. Snips expire on their own. No network calls, no telemetry.

### 🕐 LinkedIn Post Date Extractor

> **Ticket #003** &nbsp;·&nbsp; **The problem:** LinkedIn only shows relative times like "3d ago", never the exact moment a post went up.
>
> ![Status: resolved](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-resolved-2ea44f?style=flat-square)

A Chrome extension that turns LinkedIn's vague "2h ago" into a precise timestamp, decoded straight from the post ID. Useful when the order of events actually matters.
